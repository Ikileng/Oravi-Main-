Oravi: Product Requirements Document
Version 0.1 (working draft) | October 15, 2026 | Owner: Ikileng (Bradley Ikileng Etyang)
How to read this document. Every significant statement carries a status. [Decided] means settled for now. [Assumed] means we are building on it, but it must be tested. [Open] means undecided, with options listed. Numeric targets are targets until measured. This is a living draft meant to be revised after research and hospital visits.
1. Summary
Oravi keeps unfinished clinical business from getting lost when care passes between people and shifts. Today, what a nurse knows about a patient (what is pending, what changed, what to watch, what was asked of a doctor) travels through memory, a paper counter-book, verbal recap, and WhatsApp. It is routinely lost at shift boundaries.
Oravi's central idea [Decided]: tie communication and responsibility to the clinical issue, not to the person or the shift. Every unfinished item is an open loop with an owner, a conversation, and a state. Loops do not expire at shift change. They transfer, and they persist until someone explicitly closes them. A deterministic flagging layer surfaces loops that are overdue, unacknowledged, or at risk of being dropped. Intelligence beyond rules (AI drafts, risk flags) is a later phase, introduced only after it is validated.
Oravi is built for Kenyan and wider African wards: offline-first, low-cost, usable on the devices clinicians already own, in English and Kiswahili, and fitting existing workflow rather than adding a parallel one.
2. Problem and evidence
Problem statement [Decided]. When a shift changes, or when a question passes between a nurse and a doctor, critical information and responsibility do not reliably cross the boundary, and that gap is where avoidable harm occurs.
What we know.
Handoff communication failure is a widely documented source of avoidable harm in hospitals worldwide, which is why structured protocols such as SBAR and I-PASS exist.
In interviews with twelve nurses, all twelve confirmed handoff communication is a real challenge, and seven of twelve said a suitable digital tool would help and that they would embrace it. The Kardex concept came directly from these interviews.
What we do not yet know [Open].
How handoff actually runs, minute by minute, on target wards (who, where, how long, what is written versus said).
Whether dropped loops are the dominant failure, or whether staffing, missing information, or physician availability matter more.
Why five of twelve nurses were not clearly in favor, and whether the sample (small, nurse-only, limited sites) generalizes.
Whether a ward would choose Oravi over WhatsApp plus the counter-book, and what it would have to give up.
Baselines for handoff duration and missed items.
3. Vision and design principles
Vision. Nothing that matters falls through the cracks when care passes between nurses, between doctors, between departments, or from the ambulance to the ER.
Principles [Decided].
Issue over schedule. Conversations and responsibility attach to the clinical issue.
Ownership is explicit. No open loop is ever unowned.
Silence is never ambiguous. The system distinguishes 'nothing pending' (confirmed) from 'nothing recorded'.
No parallel workflow. Entry happens at moments clinicians already document, in one or two taps, mirroring the counter-book they know.
Offline by default. Core functions never depend on the internet.
Explainable intelligence. Every flag says why it fired. Humans own every clinical decision.
Honest limits. Oravi protects what is entered. It states this plainly.
Built for the setting. Low cost, cheap devices, local languages, local workflow.

5. Scope
In scope for v1 [Decided]: ward board, open-loop lifecycle, loop threads, ownership and handoff sessions, escalation chain, doctor inbox, oversight view, rule-based flags, compiled (non-AI) structured handoff summary with print, audit trail, offline operation, English and Kiswahili. Build order: nurse capabilities first, then the Ask Oravi agent. Both are tested on hospital visits before other roles are added (section 4A).
Later phases [Assumed]: other handoff contexts (physician sign-out, department-to-department transfers, paramedic pre-alert; see section 4A), the patient-scoped Ask Oravi agent (section 8A), AI-assisted handoff drafts, pattern and risk flags, voice input, photo attachments, integration with existing systems, multi-ward doctor views, analytics.
Non-goals [Decided]: Oravi is not an EHR or EMR, not a general-purpose chat app, not a diagnostic or treatment-recommending tool in v1, not a billing or claims system, and not a replacement for the patient chart.
6. Core concept: the open loop
Loop types [Decided]: (a) Task or pending result, (b) Question or clarification, (c) Escalation, (d) Consult request, (e) Risk flag (rule-raised or manual).
Urgency [Assumed]: Routine, Urgent, Critical. Names and definitions to be agreed with clinicians.
Each loop has: patient (bed plus a second identifier), type, urgency, owner (person and role), thread, state, timestamps, and full event history.
States [Decided]: Raised, Acknowledged, In progress, Resolved, Closed. Reopening is allowed with a reason. Critical loops pass through one extra state, Closed awaiting confirmation (see Critical loops below).
Ownership rules [Decided]. A loop always has an owner. If the owner is unassigned or goes off shift, it transfers at handoff or defaults to the nurse-in-charge. It never lapses.
Opening and closing rules [Decided]. Anyone with access to the patient (clinical roles, not administrative roles) can open a thread or loop, and anyone with the same access can close one. Closing always requires a reason: one tap from a short list that depends on the thread type, plus a brief note that is required for 'medication given', 'action taken', and 'other'. Each closure is recorded as resolved or closed without resolution, with who, when, and why. When a thread closes, its opener and owner are notified at once with a one-tap Reopen, and closures of Urgent and Critical items appear in the next handoff as recently closed. Nothing is ever deleted. When someone opens a new thread, Oravi shows open threads on the same patient with similar topics so they can add to one instead of creating a duplicate, and every new thread gets a default owner.
Critical loops [Decided]. Routine and Urgent loops close instantly with a reason and notify the opener and owner. Critical (STAT) loops also close instantly, but move to Closed, awaiting confirmation until the opener or a senior on duty taps Confirm or Reopen. The confirmation request quotes the closer's reason (for example: Nurse B closed your STAT heparin loop, 'Heparin running at 1000u/hr'). If nobody confirms within a set time, it passes to the next doctor or the nurse-in-charge and appears in the oversight view. Critical must stay rare and strictly defined so these requests stay meaningful.
Reopen or link [Assumed]. If the same issue recurs soon after closure, the default is to reopen the existing thread so the whole story stays together. If the cause differs or the closure was long ago, the default is a new thread linked to the earlier one. The default window is 24 hours and configurable. When starting, Oravi shows both options along with the earlier closure reason. Both routes count toward Ask Oravi pattern detection.
Persistence [Decided]. Loops persist across shifts, device changes, and discharge until closed. Discharge requires every open loop to be resolved or explicitly carried forward with an owner.
7. Functional requirements
7.1 Ward board and patients
FR-1 The home screen is a ward board with one row per bed, in a configurable order matching the ward round, showing open-loop count and the most urgent state. [Decided]
FR-2 Minimal patient record: bed, name or initials, age, sex, admission number, short admitting note, key alerts (free text, for example, allergies). Admit, transfer, and discharge are events. [Decided]
FR-3 Two patient identifiers are shown on every loop, escalation, and handoff item. [Decided]
7.2 Loops and threads
FR-4 Create a loop in two taps or fewer from a short menu of common templates, with sensible defaults for type, urgency, and owner. [Decided]
FR-5 State transitions follow section 6 and are logged with author and time. [Decided]
FR-6 Each loop has a thread of append-only messages. Edits show history. Quick-reply chips reduce typing. [Decided]
FR-7 Role mentions (for example, the doctor on call) resolve to the person currently holding that role. [Decided]
FR-8 Anyone newly assigned to a loop inherits its full history. [Decided]
FR-9 Read and acknowledgment are tracked per person. [Decided]
FR-10 Photo attachments (for example, a chart photo): [Open], deferred pending privacy and storage decisions.
FR-45 Any clinician with access to a patient can open a thread or loop. Administrative roles cannot. [Decided]
FR-46 Any clinician with access to a patient can close a thread. Closing requires a reason chosen from a short list that depends on the thread type (for a task or result thread, for example: medication given, result received, doctor reviewed, duplicate or created in error, other), with a brief free-text note required for 'medication given', 'action taken', and 'other'. Chips never carry clinical judgment (for example, 'result received', not 'result normal'). Voice dictation through the device keyboard is supported. [Decided]
FR-47 Each closure is logged with who, when, why, and whether the issue was resolved or closed without resolution. [Decided]
FR-48 On closure, the opener and owner are notified with a one-tap Reopen. Closures of Urgent and Critical items appear in the next handoff as recently closed. [Decided]
FR-49 When a thread is opened, Oravi suggests open threads on the same patient with similar topics, and every new thread receives a default owner. [Assumed]
FR-50 Closed threads are never deleted. The full open, close, and reopen history stays attached to the thread and feeds the pattern counts used by Ask Oravi. [Decided]
FR-51 A closed Critical loop enters 'Closed, awaiting confirmation' until the opener or a senior on duty confirms or reopens it. The request quotes the closer's reason. [Decided]
FR-52 An unconfirmed Critical closure escalates after a configurable time to the next doctor or the nurse-in-charge and appears in the oversight view. [Assumed]
FR-53 When an issue recurs, the user chooses Reopen or New linked thread. The default is reopen if the topic matches and the closure was within the window (24 hours by default, configurable). Linked threads display the chain. [Assumed]
FR-54 A Call action initiated from a loop thread or message logs a CallLogged event. Upon call completion or dismissal, Oravi prompts for a mandatory one-line outcome note (e.g., 'Agreed to hold dose') which is appended to the loop thread to eliminate unrecorded verbal silos. [Decided]
FR-55 Clinical roles and cadres are visually color-coded throughout threads and the ward board (e.g., Nurses in clinical Blue, Doctors/Consultants in Pink/Rose, Pharmacy/Lab in distinct hues) so scanning clinicians instantly recognize who authored an entry. [Decided]

FR-56 The Call action initiates an in-app VoIP voice call between enrolled hospital users using LiveKit WebRTC, without revealing personal phone numbers. [Decided]
FR-57 Inbound calls display an incoming-call screen and ring like a phone call via high-priority push data messages, including full-screen notification over locked screens on supported devices. [Assumed]
FR-58 If a call is unanswered after ~30 seconds or the callee is unreachable, the caller is offered an urgent message on the loop thread or escalation, and a missed-call event is anchored to the patient and loop. [Assumed]
FR-59 Call audio is strictly real-time and never recorded. Server events record call metadata (caller, callee, loop, timestamp, duration, outcome: answered, missed, declined) for audit logging. [Decided]

8. Intelligence layer (phased)
Phase 1, in v1 [Decided]: rules only. See FR-25 to FR-28. Low risk, explainable, immediately useful.
Phase 2, future [Assumed]: AI-assisted handoff draft. The model reads structured entries and notes and produces a readable SBAR draft. Design:
The original AI draft is locked and hashed. It is stored for audit and never edited.
The clinician reviews an editable copy. Each edit is a logged revision with author and diff.
The submitted version points to one specific revision and is signed by the submitting clinician, who owns the final text.
Each sentence links to the entries it came from. Deterministic checks verify that every drug, dose, number, and bed in the draft appears in the source data and flag anything unsupported.
Medication and dose fields require explicit confirmation to discourage rubber-stamping.
The incoming nurse sees the submitted version labeled as AI-assisted and reviewed by a named person. The raw original is for audit only.
Requests go through a server component only. Identifiers are minimized or pseudonymized. If the service is unreachable, the compiled template summary is used, and the ward never waits on the cloud.
Open items: cross-border data-transfer rules, provider retention terms (target zero retention), per-call cost.
Phase 3, future [Open]: pattern and risk flagging. Only after sufficient validated data, clinical governance, and a regulatory classification check. Output must show evidence, never be the only record, and never recommend treatment without an approved pathway.
Existing prototype work [Open]. The current prototype includes Claude-based patient reports and High, Medium, Low risk flags. These should be treated as experimental and off by default until validated, and reconciled with this phasing.
8A. Ask Oravi: the patient-scoped queryable agent
Purpose [Decided]. What A knows, B and C also know. The patient is the main object. A clinician who opens a patient and asks a question in plain language gets a timestamped report built only from what has been said and logged about that patient inside Oravi, in the speakers' own words, with a one-tap way to reach the person who said it. Nobody is left out of the context because they were not part of a conversation. The promise is zero fabricated quotes, not completeness: the agent reports what is logged in Oravi and always shows what it searched.
Sources and boundaries
Source [Decided]. Only Oravi's own record for that patient: handoff logs and previous handoff reports, loops opened and closed, thread messages and conversations from opening to closing, escalations and replies, and clinician-entered notes. No outside data, no general medical knowledge presented as fact about the patient, and no external EHR charting. Extending the agent's knowledge through FHIR or EHR integration is a possible future step and is out of scope for now.
Patient-scoped [Decided]. A query runs inside one patient's record. The agent never mixes in another patient's information.
Language of the record [Decided]. Quoted text is the exact wording used, in whatever language it was written, with author, role, date, time, and thread. Example: 'spo2 dropped to 63', Tue 27/10/2026, Nurse A.
Not a clinician [Decided]. The agent does not diagnose, predict, or recommend treatment. It retrieves, orders, counts, and highlights.
Visibility [Decided]. Everything said in a patient's threads is visible to anyone with access to that patient, including clinicians who were not in the conversation. Private one-to-one messages are not part of the agent's source. A message sent through a quote's Message button is created as a thread on the patient and therefore becomes part of the record.
Behavior rules
Quote, do not paraphrase. The report is assembled from verbatim snippets. Any wording the system adds is minimal, clearly marked as Oravi's, and links to the quotes behind it.
Show, do not judge. If an entry looks odd (for example 'spo2 dropped to 63' followed minutes later by 'recheck spo2 93'), show both with times and authors. Never decide which is right.
Say what was not found. If nothing matches, say 'no record in Oravi' (never 'no record exists'), list the search terms and the time range, and state that absence of a mention is not evidence that the event did not happen or that all is well.
Show coverage. Every answer ends with what was searched: number of messages and threads, and the time span.
Surface conflicts. If two entries disagree, show both. Do not reconcile them.
Separate record from observation. Counts and patterns computed by the system (a thread reopened 4 times) are labeled as system counts. Any model-written observation is labeled 'Note from Oravi, not from the record' and cites its evidence.
Every quote is actionable. Each quote has buttons to message or call the author, or the current owner of the patient if the author is unreachable.
How the agent is made smart
'Smart' here means three things: it finds everything relevant, it quotes it faithfully, and it knows when it found nothing. It does not mean clever clinical reasoning. The design below is built for that.
Patient-scoped retrieval. Every message and event is indexed per patient with metadata (author, role, time, thread, loop state). A query combines keyword search with semantic search so that paraphrases and misspellings are still found. [Assumed]
A ward lexicon. Build a vocabulary of terms, abbreviations, and local usage from real ward language: urine, UO, passing urine, mkojo; SpO2, sats, oxygen; and so on, across English, Kiswahili, and mixed speech. Grow it from the failures found in testing. [Assumed]
Time is handled by code, not by the model. Windows such as 'last 8 hours' or 'since Monday' are computed by deterministic code using the system clock. Sorting and counting are also code. The model never does date arithmetic. [Decided]
Quote-first generation. The model's job is to choose which snippets answer the question and in what order. The final report is built from the original text. [Decided]
Automatic verification. Before display, check that every quoted string appears exactly in the record, that every number in any model-written text appears in a cited quote, and that every cited author and time match. Any failure blocks the answer or removes the offending line. [Decided]
Deterministic pattern detectors. Count repeated openings and closings of a thread, repeated escalations on one topic, the same topic raised across several shifts, and long silences on an open concern. These are cheap, explainable, and safe. A model may suggest further possible patterns, but they are labeled and evidenced. [Assumed]
Long histories. For patients with long records, keep per-shift digests built from verbatim snippets with links to sources, so answers stay fast and traceable. Digests are never a substitute for the record. [Assumed]
Boundary handling. Questions that ask for a diagnosis, prediction, or treatment get a clear refusal plus the relevant record and a button to ask the doctor in charge. [Decided]
Learning from use. A 'Something is missing' button on every answer logs the miss for review. Misses feed the lexicon, the detectors, and the test set. [Assumed]
Model choice [Open]. A strong hosted model through a server component, or a smaller model nearer the ward. Criteria: accuracy on the test set, cost per query, latency, offline behavior, and data-protection obligations (identifier minimization, transfer rules, retention terms).
Requirements
FR-34 A clinician can open a patient and ask a question in plain language. The agent answers for that patient only. [Decided]
FR-35 Answers consist of verbatim quotes with author, role, date, time, and thread, ordered by time. [Decided]
FR-36 Every answer includes a coverage line stating terms searched, number of messages and threads, and the time span. [Decided]
FR-37 If nothing is found, the answer says 'no record in Oravi' (never 'no record exists') and states that absence of a mention is not evidence of absence. [Decided]
FR-38 Each quote offers message and call buttons for its author, with a fallback to the patient's current owner if the author is off shift. A message sent this way is created as a clarification loop anchored to the quoted message. [Assumed]
FR-39 System-computed patterns (reopen counts, repeated escalations, recurring topics) are shown with timestamps and labeled as system counts. They state counts and show the quotes behind them, never a clinical meaning (a fever thread reopened 3 times is reported as a count with its quotes, not as a sign of infection). Numeric trend statements, such as urine values falling across shifts, wait for a later phase; until then, the values are shown side by side as quotes. Patterns appear in answers when someone asks and as a quiet note on the patient screen. They do not trigger push alerts in v1. [Assumed]
FR-40 Model-written observations are labeled, cite their quotes, and never include diagnosis, prediction, or treatment advice. [Decided]
FR-41 The agent understands English, Kiswahili, and mixed-language questions and records, and replies in the language of the question. [Assumed]
FR-42 Access to the agent follows the same permissions as access to the patient's record. Every query is logged with user, patient, time, and question. [Decided]
FR-43 The deterministic parts (patient timeline, search, counts) work offline. The natural-language layer needs a connection or ward server and degrades to timeline and search when unavailable. [Assumed]
FR-44 A 'Something is missing' button on every answer records feedback for review. [Assumed]

10. Non-functional requirements
Offline-first [Decided]. All core actions work with no internet and are persisted locally. Sync runs automatically when a connection exists. Principle: never silently discard a clinical entry. When two entries conflict, both are preserved and flagged for human resolution. Deterministic merge rules are specified in the design document.
Performance targets [Assumed, to validate on real devices]: board opens in under two seconds on a target device. Local actions confirm instantly. Sync within about ten seconds on a working ward network.
Devices [Assumed]: Android on entry-level and mid-range phones that clinicians already own or that wards provide. Exact minimum specification to be set after a device survey, not assumed.
Security. Per-user authentication, role-based access, encryption at rest on device and server and in transit, auto-lock, remote wipe for lost devices, no patient information in notification previews, minimal information in SMS, per-device enrollment.
Privacy and data protection. Operate in line with the Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019 and obligations under the Digital Health Act, 2023. To confirm with counsel: controller and processor roles, registration, lawful basis and consent, data residency and cross-border transfer, retention periods. [Open]
Auditability. Tamper-evident (hash-chained) log, with deletion and update of records disabled at the data layer.
Accessibility and usability. Large tap targets, high contrast, a dark mode for night shifts, status conveyed by shape and text as well as color, glanceable under poor lighting.
Reliability. Defined behavior for each failure mode in section 13.
11. Data model overview
Entities [Assumed]: Facility, Ward, Bed, Patient and Encounter, User, RoleAssignment (time-ranged), Shift, Loop, LoopEvent (append-only), Message, HandoffSession, HandoffAcceptance, Escalation, Flag, AuditEvent, and later DraftDocument and DraftRevision.
Principles [Decided]: loop state is derived from append-only events. Event identifiers are generated on the client so retries after a dropped connection are idempotent. Role assignments are time-ranged records so 'who was responsible at 02:10?' is a simple query. The model is shaped to map cleanly to FHIR resources even though v1 does not integrate with other systems.
12. Architecture direction
Standalone-first, integration-ready [Decided]. Oravi works on a ward with no EMR, while keeping data shapes compatible so a hospital with an existing system can connect later.
Deployment model [Open]. Options: (A) cloud service with offline-capable clients; (B) a small on-premises device per ward that all phones sync with; (C) hybrid, with a ward device that also syncs to the cloud. Decision criteria: cost per ward, failure modes, support burden, real connectivity, data-protection posture, and ward Wi-Fi behavior. Decide after site visits. Clients are built local-first with a sync interface that does not presume the choice.
Integration [Assumed]. Later: import patient lists and orders, export FHIR-shaped data. Embedding in existing EMRs through SMART on FHIR or CDS Hooks only where the host system supports it, which must be verified for the systems actually used. Heavier clinical data repository standards such as openEHR are not required for v1. [Open]
AI-ready seams [Decided]. A single draft-generation interface behind a feature flag: today it is the template compiler, later it may be a model. Every future AI step is marked in design notes.
Core stack [Decided]. PostgreSQL is the system of record. Drift on SQLite, encrypted with SQLCipher, is the device store. Flutter is the client framework. Firebase Realtime Database is retired as the main store. FCM push notifications stay. Whether to keep Firebase Auth or use Oravi's own login is open.
Event log as source of truth [Decided]. State (loop status, ownership) is derived from append-only events with client-generated IDs, so retries after a dropped connection are safe.
Sync layer [Open]. Decided by a time-boxed spike comparing (1) a purpose-built append-only event-log sync with deterministic conflict handling on the backend and (2) PowerSync, evaluating its licensing, self-hosting footprint, and how custom upload and conflict logic is handled.
Hosting and data residency [Open]. Whether the Digital Health Act 2023, the Data Protection Act 2019, or hospital procurement rules require in-country or on-premise hosting must be verified with counsel, not assumed. If in-country hosting is required, evaluate Nairobi-based providers (for example Safaricom Cloud and Africa Data Centres, whose actual offerings, pricing, and certifications are still to be checked). Self-hostability is kept as an architectural asset for flexibility.
Ward hub footprint [Assumed]. If an on-premise hub is used: one compiled service plus plain PostgreSQL on a low-power fanless PC, avoiding heavy container stacks.
Audit integrity [Decided]. Append-only event tables, revoked UPDATE and DELETE grants, a hash chain across events, and periodic signed exports kept off the server. Administrators with database access could still alter data, so the exports are what make tampering detectable.
Search and embeddings [Assumed]. Keyword plus semantic search (pgvector) within one patient's small record, mainly for synonym and English and Kiswahili paraphrase matching, not scale. Prefer a small self-hosted embedding model to avoid sending patient text to outside APIs, and test it on real Kiswahili ward phrases. Keyword search with the ward lexicon is the fallback.
Operations [Assumed]. Backups with tested restores, monitoring, upgrades, and key management, using a managed instance nearby where permitted.
Client robustness [Assumed]. Drift migrations that stay safe when phones run different app versions, and tested background sync and alert behavior on Transsion and Xiaomi phones, which often restrict background apps.
Detail lives elsewhere [Decided]. This section records decisions, constraints, and open questions. Schemas, hash-chain specifications, migration mechanics, and vendor comparisons belong in a separate technical architecture document.
